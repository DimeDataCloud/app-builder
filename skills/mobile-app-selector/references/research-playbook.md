# Research Playbook — Mobile App Selector

The two objective gates (Demand / Differentiability) are only as good as the research behind them. This playbook is how you turn a candidate concept into an honest 1-5 score instead of an optimistic guess. Run this for each top candidate before scoring.

Target: 3-5 searches per candidate. You are looking for signal, not writing a market report. Bring one concrete finding to each gate when you score it.

---

## Gate 1 — Demand

The question: does a real, painful problem exist for this specific mobile user, are they complaining about it publicly, and are they already spending on adjacent solutions?

**Search shapes** (swap in the audience, problem, and any competitor names):

- `[audience] app reddit` — look for complaint threads, rant posts, "is there an app for this" requests
- `[problem] mobile app "wish there was"` — surfaces explicit demand
- `[competitor app name] reviews` — App Store reviews (1-3 stars), Google Play reviews, Trustpilot
- `[audience] [problem] forum` / `[audience] subreddit` — community discussion of the pain
- `[competitor name] alternatives` — see what users are actively looking for instead

**How to read it:**

- **Score 5** — Multiple active threads where this specific audience complains about this specific problem. Competitors exist with 100+ reviews and users clearly paying for something adjacent. Budget and problem awareness are both confirmed.
- **Score 3** — Some complaint threads but sparse or old (12+ months). One competitor with moderate engagement. Problem is recognized but not top of mind.
- **Score 1 (VETO)** — No community discussion. No complaints. No competitors. Either the problem doesn't exist at scale, or the audience hasn't developed awareness. Score conservatively and flag this clearly.

**The "no competitors" read:**

No competitors can mean two different things:
- No competitors + active complaints = real gap. People have the problem and no one has built the solution. High potential.
- No competitors + no complaints = likely not a real problem at scale, or you're searching the wrong terms. Don't interpret low competition as opportunity without complaint evidence.

**Concrete finding to bring to scoring:**

"Found a 4K-member subreddit with weekly posts about exactly this friction — score 4."

Or: "Searched 5 ways and found no complaints, no forum threads, no competitor reviews mentioning this pain. Scoring a 2 until there's evidence."

---

## Gate 3 — Differentiability

The question: is the App Store market dominated by well-reviewed incumbents, or is there a real crack where a focused app can win?

**Search shapes:**

- `best [concept] app` / `[concept] app [audience]` — what's already in the space
- `[concept] app alternatives` / `[concept] app competitor` — what users are comparing
- Direct App Store search for the core keyword — note the top 3 results, their ratings, review counts, and last-update dates
- `[top competitor] reviews problems` / `[top competitor] "doesn't work for"` — surfaces the incumbent's gap
- `[top competitor name] last update` — stale products signal opportunity

**How to read it:**

- **Score 5** — The existing apps are old (last meaningful update pre-2021), generic (designed for all industries, not this audience), or poorly reviewed (under 3.5 stars on average with complaints about exactly the gap this app fills). Clear white space for a focused product.
- **Score 3** — Competitors exist and work reasonably. Solid reviews. But there is a persistent gap in reviews, a specific user type they ignore, or a feature set that's consistently missing. Winnable with sharp positioning.
- **Score 1 (VETO)** — A well-funded, well-reviewed, dominant app already serves exactly this audience for exactly this problem. Updated regularly. High ratings. Building a "me too" product here is not a strategy.

**Recency matters:**

A competitor last updated in 2019 with no recent reviews is a different signal than one updated last month with active reviews. Old tools with active complaints are prime targets.

**Concrete finding to bring to scoring:**

"Found two competitors: [App A] (4.1 stars, 1,200 reviews, last updated 8 months ago, top complaint is no offline mode) and [App B] (2.8 stars, 90 reviews, clearly abandoned). The gap is clear. Score 4."

Or: "[App X] dominates this space — 4.6 stars, 8,000 reviews, updated monthly, serves exactly this user. No obvious gap. Score 2."

---

## Reading Both Scores Together

Demand and Differentiability interact. Use this read:

- **Demand 5 + Differentiability 5** — Best case: proven pain, no one has solved it well for this audience. Build it.
- **Demand 5 + Differentiability 2** — Real pain, dominated market. Look for the gap in the incumbent's reviews before giving up — sometimes there's a niche angle that's winnable.
- **Demand 2 + Differentiability 5** — Open space with no proven pain. Low competition doesn't make it worth building. Find the complaints first.
- **Demand 2 + Differentiability 2** — Nothing here. Move to the next candidate.

---

## Honesty Rules

- Never invent review counts, download estimates, or forum activity. If you can't find it, say so and score conservatively.
- Download estimates: use review count × ~100 as a rough proxy. It's imprecise and can be off by a factor of 10, so flag it as an estimate — "roughly 50K-500K installs based on review count."
- A concept that vetoes on either gate is dead regardless of how elegant the idea is. Surface it clearly and fast — it saves weeks.
- 3-5 searches is the target. Don't spiral into a full market analysis — that's mobile-industry-download's job once the concept is locked.
- Recency: note when a competitor was last updated. A stale app with complaints is opportunity. A freshly updated app with high ratings is a wall.
