# Mobile Discovery Script

The exact questions to ask so the person on the other end of the call tells you whether the problem is real, whether mobile is the right format, and whether they'd pay for or use what you're building.

## What This Skill Does

**Input:** A mobile app concept (the locked sentence from mobile-app-selector) + whether this is consumer (App Store) or B2B mobile (enterprise)

**Output:** A structured 15-20 minute call script — opener, context questions, problem-digging questions, mobile-specific validation questions, and a soft close. Tailored to consumer user interview mode or enterprise sales mode.

You don't pitch on this call. You ask. They talk. You find out if the problem is real enough to matter.

## The Core Principle

**The person you're talking to should be speaking 70% of the time. You are asking, listening, and taking notes.**

Your job is not to convince anyone of anything. It's to find out:
1. Does this problem exist for them specifically?
2. Do they experience it on their phone, not just on a computer?
3. How much does it cost them (time, money, or a goal they fail to achieve)?
4. What have they already tried?

If those four answers are strong, proceed. If they're weak, the problem or the format is wrong.

---

## Two Modes

This skill outputs in one of two modes depending on whether the app is consumer or enterprise B2B.

---

## Mode 1: Consumer User Interview

**Goal:** Validate that the pain exists, that mobile is the right format, and that this specific person would download and use the app. Close on a beta test invite.

**Not a sales call.** You're not trying to close money. You're trying to find out if you're building for a real problem.

---

### Call Frame (Verbatim Opener)

> "Thanks for taking a few minutes. I'm building a mobile app and I want to ask you some questions about how you currently handle [general problem area]. I'm not going to pitch you anything — I just want to understand what it's actually like. Sound good?"

This frame does three things: sets expectations (no pitch), establishes you're listening (not selling), and gets them in question-answer mode.

---

### Context Questions (2-3 minutes)

Light warm-up. Get them describing their situation before you dig into pain.

1. "Tell me a little about [the context the app addresses] — what does that look like for you day to day?"
2. "How often do you deal with [the problem area] in a typical week?"
3. "What apps do you currently use for [this category]? What do you like about them?"

---

### Problem-Digging Questions (5-8 minutes)

Funnel from broad to specific. Find the friction moment.

1. "What's the most annoying part of [the workflow or activity] right now?"
2. "Walk me through the last time [the specific friction moment] happened. What were you doing, and what broke down?"
3. "What do you do when [the app/tool/workaround] fails you? What's the backup?"
4. "How often does that workaround actually work? What falls through the cracks?"
5. "If you had to guess — how much time do you lose per week because of this?"

**What to listen for in consumer mode:**
- Green: They describe a specific moment, specific cost, specific workaround. The problem is real.
- Yellow: They acknowledge the problem but can't recall a specific instance. May not be painful enough.
- Red: "It's not really a big deal" / "I've just gotten used to it." Not a real problem.

---

### Mobile-Specific Validation (3-4 minutes)

This is what makes consumer discovery different from general product research. You're building a mobile app — you need to know if mobile is actually the right format.

1. "When you deal with [this problem], are you usually on your phone or on a computer?"
2. "Have you ever tried to handle this on your phone? What happened?"
3. "If there was an app that [core value prop in one sentence], would that be useful on your phone or would you only use it on a laptop?"
4. "What would make you actually open the app every day vs. download it and forget about it?"

**What to listen for:**
- Green: "I'm always on my phone when this happens" / "I tried to do it on mobile but it was terrible"
- Yellow: "Sometimes phone, sometimes computer depending on where I am"
- Red: "I'd only use something like that at my desk" — this is a web app, not a mobile app

---

### Soft Close — Consumer Mode (1-2 minutes)

> "This has been really helpful. I'm building exactly this — an app that [one sentence]. I'm looking for a handful of people to test the first version. Would you be up for that?"

If yes: "Great. What's the best way to reach you when it's ready?"

If no: "No worries. Can I ask — is it the concept that doesn't fit, or just that you don't use beta apps?"

**Note:** The close is not "would you pay for this?" — that question at this stage produces unreliable answers. The real signal is whether they want to test it.

---

### Listen-For Cheat Sheet — Consumer Mode

**Green flags (real pain, real mobile need):**
- Describes a specific moment of failure, not a general frustration
- "I wish there was an app for this"
- "I keep switching between [App A] and [App B] to do this one thing"
- Uses their phone for this already, even if the experience is bad
- Can estimate time lost per week

**Yellow flags (probe further):**
- "It's a little annoying but not a big deal"
- "I use [desktop app] for that"
- Can't recall a specific recent instance of the problem

**Red flags (wrong user or wrong format):**
- "I've never really thought about that as a problem"
- "I'd only use that at my desk"
- "We already have a system for that that works fine"

## Mode 2: Enterprise Sales Call

**Goal:** Find out whether the business has a specific mobile workflow problem, quantify the cost, and close on a proposal to deploy the app to their team.

**This is a sales call.** You're looking for a decision-maker who can authorize a deployment. The close is a proposal, not a beta invite.

---

### Call Frame (Verbatim Opener)

> "Thanks for your time. I want to ask you a few questions about how your [field team / staff / specific role] handles [general workflow area]. I'm not going to pitch you today — I just want to understand the situation. If what I'm building sounds like a fit, I'll put together a specific proposal. Sound good?"

---

### Rapport Questions (2-3 minutes)

Business-relevant warm-up. Get them describing their operation before you dig into pain.

1. "How many people are on your [relevant team]?"
2. "How do they currently handle [the workflow in question] — what tools are they using?"
3. "How long have you been running the operation this way?"

---

### Problem-Finding Questions (5-8 minutes)

Funnel from broad to specific. Find the workflow that's broken.

1. "What's the biggest mobile or field communication challenge your team deals with right now?"
2. "Walk me through what happens when [the specific friction scenario] occurs. What breaks down?"
3. "What do they do in the moment when [the app/tool] doesn't work? What's the workaround?"
4. "How often does that workaround fail? What falls through the cracks when it does?"
5. "What does it cost you when [the specific failure] happens?" (Push for a dollar number if they hedge.)

**What to listen for:**
- Green: They can describe a specific workflow failure and quantify the cost (time, lost revenue, compliance risk)
- Yellow: They know there's a problem but haven't tracked the cost. Worth probing: "If you had to guess at the monthly dollar impact, what would you say?"
- Red: "IT controls what apps our staff can install" / "We're locked into [enterprise software] for mobile" / "Our MDM policies don't allow new apps"

---

### Mobile-Specific Questions (2-3 minutes)

1. "Is this something your team deals with in the field, or primarily at a desk?"
2. "Have you tried solving this with a mobile app before? What happened?"
3. "What would make your team actually use a new app vs. going back to [their current workaround]?"
4. "Who else on your team would need to be involved in a decision like this?"

---

### Dollar Quantification (3-5 minutes)

Don't leave without a number.

1. "How much staff time per week goes to [the broken workflow]?"
2. "What's the average [cost per incident / hourly rate / customer impact] when this breaks down?"
3. "If this was solved, what would change first? What's the most immediate dollar impact?"

---

### Soft Close — Enterprise Mode (2-3 minutes)

> "Based on what you've described — [paraphrase their problem and cost] — I think I can build something specific for your team. Let me put together a proposal showing exactly what I'd deploy, how it would work for [their specific workflow], and what the investment looks like. Could we schedule 30 minutes next week to walk through it?"

If yes: confirm time.
If no: "Can I ask what would make a proposal worth looking at? Is it the timing, the problem fit, or something else?"

---

### Common Objections — Enterprise Mode

**"IT controls what apps our staff can install"**
Reframe: "Understood. If I built this as an enterprise app distributed through your MDM, would that path be open? Or is this a policy question I'd need to address separately?"

**"We already have [software X] for that"**
Reframe: "What does [software X] do well for this? And what gaps do your people still work around?" (Probe for what the existing tool doesn't cover — that's often the opening.)

**"We're not ready to add new tools right now"**
Reframe: "What would need to be true for timing to work better? Is it a budget cycle, a project milestone, or something else?"

---

### Listen-For Cheat Sheet — Enterprise Mode

**Green flags:**
- Describes a specific mobile workflow failure with a dollar cost or staff hours attached
- "I wish there was an app for this" / "We've looked for something and couldn't find it"
- Decision-maker is on the call (or introduces you to one)
- "Our staff is already asking for something like this"

**Red flags:**
- "IT controls everything on our devices"
- "We're locked into [enterprise software suite] for all mobile tools"
- No quantifiable cost attached to the problem
- Vague frustration with no specific failure moment

---

## After the Call

Log the key answers:
- Did the problem come up clearly?
- Was mobile confirmed as the right format?
- What was the estimated cost / time loss?
- What's the next step?

If consumer: note whether they agreed to beta test, and their contact.
If enterprise: send the proposal within 48 hours. Reference their specific numbers.
